# Website wording for the demo sounds, one "Preset Name = text" per line.
#
# These are for a visitor browsing the site, not for the manual. The preset's
# own description stays in the preset file and stays technical -- it is
# documentation and says what was measured. This says what it sounds like.
# A preset with no line here falls back to its own description.
# Read by shared/tools/make-demos.sh.

City Thunder = Thunder between buildings, the way a phone catches it. The blast lands first, the claps wander over the rooftops, then the street shakes.
Close Strike = A strike a few hundred metres off: a tearing crack, then a long roll with the hills answering it.
Cloud Crawler = Lightning crawling along the underside of the cloud. Nothing reaches the ground, and the whole sky rolls for half a minute.
Deep Sub = The cinematic one. Long, heavy and felt in the floor rather than heard.
Distant Rumble = A storm ten kilometres away. No crack at all, just a slow deep roll swelling in.
Dry Crack = Short and sharp. One stroke, no roll, no echo.
Far Horizon = Lightning on the horizon, twenty kilometres out. More felt than heard, and it takes its time arriving.
Gated Rumble = The thunder lasts exactly as long as you hold the key. Short notes for stabs, long ones for the whole roll.
Heat Lightning = A storm too far off to see properly. The odd flash every quarter minute, barely a murmur with it.
Heavy Impact = Close enough that it arrives as one blow. Dark, sudden, and over in a few seconds.
Hollow Strike = A trailer strike: the bottom and the crack land on either side of a hole in the middle. Not what a recording measures, and that is the point.
Mountain Echoes = A strike in a valley, thrown back off every wall until the echoes outlast the thunder.
Overhead Crack = Right on top of you. A short violent tear with almost nothing behind it.
Rolling Thunder = A crooked bolt a few kilometres off, its branches arriving one after another for twenty seconds.
Single Bolt = One clean bolt a kilometre away. A single stroke, a single clap, and an honest decay.
Storm Front = The squall is overhead. Flashes every few seconds, close and violent, nothing held back.
Summer Storm = Hold a note and a storm passes over: near and far flashes every ten seconds or so, none of them alike.
Under The Rain = Built to sit underneath rain. A storm seven kilometres off that never steps on anything.
