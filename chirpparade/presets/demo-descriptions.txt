# Website wording for the demo sounds, one "Preset Name = text" per line.
#
# These are for a visitor browsing the site, not for the manual. The preset's
# own description stays in the preset file and stays technical -- it is
# documentation and says what was measured. This says what it sounds like.
# A preset with no line here falls back to its own description.
# Read by shared/tools/make-demos.sh.

Alarm Flock = Short, high and fast, which is what an alarm call is for: hard to place and impossible to ignore. Seven birds, all of them agitated.
Blackbird Song = A phrase that actually goes somewhere: five notes, a pause about four times as long as the gaps inside it, then round again.
Budgie Chatter = Four budgies talking over each other. The busiest and chattiest thing in the set.
Crane Bugle = A bugle carrying across water. Far enough away that only the middle of the call arrives.
Dawn Chorus = Twelve birds over two octaves answering each other. Nothing plays on the note -- hold one and you get a morning.
Far Treeline = Birds a field away. Quieter, duller, and the fine detail smoothed off, which is what really makes them sound far rather than just soft.
Forest Morning = A wood in spring with a woodpecker somewhere in it, drumming every quarter minute or so.
Garden Sparrows = Ordinary garden birds, and the middle of everything that was measured. The place to start.
Goose Flight = Geese going over, spread out at different distances, which is most of what makes a skein sound like a skein.
Green Woodpecker = The yaffle: a falling laugh at ten notes a second, slowing as it goes.
Jungle Screech = The one with no real bird behind it. The most extreme shapes in the whole library, pushed further. No bird makes this noise, and it is here on purpose.
Melody Bird = For playing tunes with. One note per key, hardly any wandering, and playing harder sends the pitch up the way a bird calling harder does.
Nightingale Song = A nightingale never repeats itself, and neither does this. Short phrases running together, real silence between them, and no two the same.
Parakeet Flock = The same voice driven hard and made ragged. Ten of them at once is a street full of parakeets, not a cage.
Robin Whistle = The purest voice in the set: almost a plain whistle. Four notes with a slight fall between them.
Single Chirp = One bird, one note, no flock. The plugin reduced to what a single key does, and dry on purpose.
Tui Bell = A bell-like call that comes from the shape of the throat and beak rather than the voice. A short neck and a wide beak, put to work.
Woodpecker Drum = Drumming only, one roll per key. Fourteen strikes that speed up across the roll -- which is what real ones do, whatever you have read.
